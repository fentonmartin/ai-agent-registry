# Positioning Framework — Evidence-Based Messaging

> Positioning is not a tagline. It is the strategic choice of who you are for, against what
> alternatives, and why. Everything in this file must be backed by evidence. See icp-framework.md
> for evidence label definitions.

---

## 1. Positioning Statement Template

Use this format to create and stress-test positioning. Fill in each slot with evidence-backed content.

```
For [clearly defined audience — job title, company type, situation]
who [specific situation or struggle they face right now],
[Product name] is the [category: tool / platform / service / approach]
that [specific, differentiated claim — what you do differently]
because [proof: why you can make this claim — mechanism, evidence, track record].

Unlike [primary alternative: existing behavior, competitor, or status quo],
we [specific contrast — what you do that they don't or can't].
```

**Example:**
> For RevOps Managers at B2B SaaS companies scaling from 50 to 200 employees
> who are manually reconciling pipeline attribution across 3+ tools,
> Acme is the attribution layer that closes the pipeline-to-revenue loop in under 48 hours
> because it reads directly from your existing CRM and marketing automation without a data
> engineering project.
>
> Unlike BI tools that require SQL skills and weeks of setup,
> we deliver accurate attribution on day one without touching your data warehouse.

## 3. Differentiation Matrix

Score each dimension honestly. Don't claim parity as advantage.

| Dimension | Us | Alternative A | Alternative B | Status Quo |
|-----------|-----|--------------|--------------|-----------|
| [Dimension 1: e.g., Time to value] | [Claim + evidence] | [Their position] | [Their position] | [Status quo position] |
| [Dimension 2: e.g., Technical lift required] | [Claim + evidence] | [Their position] | [Their position] | [Status quo position] |
| [Dimension 3: e.g., Data accuracy] | [Claim + evidence] | [Their position] | [Their position] | [Status quo position] |
| [Dimension 4: e.g., Buyer's required skills] | [Claim + evidence] | [Their position] | [Their position] | [Status quo position] |

**Rules for the matrix:**
- Only claim differentiation where you have evidence (Verified or Inferred)
- If a competitor matches you on a dimension, mark it as parity — don't fake it
- Focus on dimensions that buyers actually evaluate in decisions (ask in win/loss interviews)
- Update when competitive landscape changes

## 4. Claim Ladder

Every feature should ladder up to a business outcome. Map claims here to enable consistent messaging.

```
Feature → Capability → Operational Change → Business Outcome
```

| Feature | Capability | Operational Change | Business Outcome |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| [Feature: e.g., CRM-native integration] | [Capability: reads live deal data without ETL] | [Op change: RevOps analyst saves 12 h/week on manual pulls] | [Outcome: faster board reporting; CEO has confidence in pipeline forecast] |
| [Feature 2] | [Capability 2] | [Op change 2] | [Outcome 2] |
| [Feature 3] | [Capability 3] | [Op change 3] | [Outcome 3] |

**Rule:** In customer-facing communication, lead with Operational Change or Business Outcome.
Lead with Feature only in technical reviews or when buyer explicitly asks "how does it work."

## 5. Message Variants by Buyer Role

One message does not fit all stakeholders. Tailor to what each role cares about.

| Role | Primary Concern | Message Focus | Proof Type to Use |
|------|----------------|--------------|------------------|
| **RevOps Manager / Director** | Reducing manual work; accuracy; team capacity | Operational efficiency; time saved; accuracy improvement | Workflow before/after; time-saved metric |
| **VP Revenue / CRO** | Pipeline confidence; forecast accuracy; hitting number | Revenue impact; faster decision-making; board-ready metrics | Pipeline visibility improvement; revenue outcome |
| **CFO / VP Finance** | ROI; payback period; cost of status quo | Cost of manual process + cost of bad decisions vs. tool cost | TCO comparison; payback calculation |
| **IT / Security** | Data residency; security; integration complexity | Simple integration; security posture; no data copies | SOC 2; architecture diagram; integration docs |
| **CEO / Founder (small company)** | Revenue growth; operational leverage; competitive position | Strategic narrative; market positioning; scalability | Customer stories; growth metrics |

**Example — same claim, different audience:**

> *For RevOps Manager:* "You stop spending 12 hours a week pulling reports and start having the answer ready when your CRO asks."

> *For CRO:* "Your forecast is grounded in real pipeline data, not a RevOps analyst's best guess assembled on Sunday night."

> *For CFO:* "You're paying [X] per year for a manual process that still produces errors. We replace that for [Y], with a documented accuracy improvement."

## 6. Proof Mapping Table

Match each claim to its strongest available proof. Never make a claim without a proof path.

| Claim | Proof Type | Evidence Available | Evidence Gap | Action if Gap |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|-------------|--------------|
| [Claim 1] | Case study | [Link or description] | None | — |
| [Claim 2] | Customer quote | "[Quote] — [Name]" | Weak proof | Get 2 more customer quotes by [date] |
| [Claim 3] | Metric | [Source + N] | Sample too small | Expand data set before using externally |
| [Claim 4] | Reference call | [Customer available] | None | — |
| [Claim 5] | No proof yet | — | Full gap | Mark as Hypothesis; do NOT use externally |

**Evidence gap rule:** If a claim has no proof, label it internally as Hypothesis and do NOT use it in customer-facing materials until evidence is gathered.

## 7. What NOT to Do

### Messaging Antipatterns — Avoid These

- **Generic pain statements** — "Companies struggle with data silos" is true of everyone and means nothing. Be specific.
- **Unsubstantiated superlatives** — "The most powerful / best / leading platform" without proof is ignored or distrusted.
- **Feature-first opening** — Starting with "we have X feature" before establishing why the buyer should care.
- **Jargon the buyer doesn't use** — If they don't say "attribution graph" in discovery, don't say it in your pitch.
- **Borrowing competitor positioning** — If you sound like them, why would a buyer switch?
- **Claiming Inferred evidence as Verified** — Destroys credibility when challenged in a deal review.
- **One-size-fits-all messaging** — Sending the CRO message to the RevOps analyst, or vice versa.
- **Overclaiming ROI** — "10x revenue" without a customer story to back it is a red flag for sophisticated buyers.
- **Ignoring the alternative** — Buyers will always have a mental comparison. Name it and address it; silence creates doubt.
- **Positioning against a competitor you can't name** — If you can't say their name in conversation, don't build positioning around them.
